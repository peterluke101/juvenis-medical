NAD+ IV Therapy:
Cellular Energy, DNA Repair, and Longevity Support

NAD+ has been called everything from a miracle anti-aging molecule to a placebo with marketing. The truth is more interesting and more useful: it's a real, well-studied coenzyme that does specific things in your cells, declines measurably with age, and can be replenished — but the benefits people actually notice depend a lot on where they're starting from.

Here's what NAD+ does, why your levels probably aren't what they used to be, and what to realistically expect from therapy.

What NAD+ Actually Is

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide — NAD+ — is a coenzyme present in every cell in your body. It plays an essential role in:

  • Energy production — converting food into ATP via mitochondrial electron transport
  • DNA repair — activating sirtuins and PARP enzymes that maintain genomic integrity
  • Cellular signaling — regulating circadian rhythm, stress response, and metabolic adaptation
  • Redox balance — managing oxidative stress at the cellular level

Without NAD+, basic cellular function stops. Low NAD+ doesn't kill you — but reduced NAD+ availability is increasingly recognized as a hallmark of biological aging.

Why NAD+ Declines

NAD+ levels in tissues drop roughly 50% between age 20 and age 50, with continued decline after that. Several mechanisms drive this:

  • Increased NAD+ consumption by enzymes (CD38, PARP1) that respond to chronic inflammation and DNA damage
  • Reduced biosynthesis as precursor enzymes lose efficiency
  • Lifestyle factors — chronic stress, poor sleep, alcohol, metabolic dysfunction — that accelerate the decline
  • Senescent cell accumulation, which is associated with elevated CD38 expression

By midlife, the gap between cellular energy supply and demand becomes noticeable in the form of declining recovery, brain fog, and the general sense that things are working less efficiently than they used to. NAD+ replenishment doesn't reverse aging — but it can address one specific contributor.

What Patients Actually Notice

01

Cleaner, More Sustained Energy

The most common report — patients describe energy as "cleaner" or "less crashy." Less reliance on caffeine. More even mid-afternoon. Better tolerance for cognitively demanding work without the brain-fog drag. Effects show up gradually over the first 2–4 weeks of consistent therapy.

02

Mental Clarity and Focus

NAD+'s role in mitochondrial function is particularly important in the brain — neurons are exceptionally energy-hungry. Patients commonly report sharper morning thinking, better word recall, and improved focus during sustained work. This is among the more reliable benefits.

03

Recovery From Training and Travel

NAD+'s role in mitochondrial recovery shows up in faster bounceback from hard training sessions, jet lag, and short sleep. Athletes often add NAD+ to their protocols during heavy training blocks or competition prep. Patients also frequently use it before/after long-distance travel.

04

Mood and Stress Tolerance

Less well-documented than energy effects, but consistently reported — patients describe improved stress tolerance, more emotional steadiness, and better mood through the day. Some of this likely reflects improved energy and sleep; some may reflect direct NAD+ involvement in neurotransmitter pathways.

05

Long-Term Cellular Health Markers

This is where NAD+ shows its longevity-oriented potential. Restored NAD+ supports sirtuin activity, which is linked to DNA repair and stress-response pathways. The clinical impact of these mechanisms over years isn't yet fully quantified in humans, but the cellular biology is well-characterized and consistent with NAD+ being part of a sound longevity protocol.

Delivery Methods: IV vs Subcutaneous

NAD+ can be delivered in several ways, each with trade-offs:

IV Infusion

The classic delivery — typically 250mg to 1000mg infused slowly over 1–4 hours. Pros: highest peak concentration, useful for intensive protocols. Cons: time-intensive, must be done in clinic, the infusion itself is uncomfortable for some patients (chest tightness, GI sensations) when given too fast.

Subcutaneous Injection

Self-administered injection, typically 50–100mg, several times per week. Pros: convenient, no clinic time, gentler on the body, allows continuous tissue exposure rather than spike-and-trough. Cons: smaller doses per administration. Increasingly the preferred maintenance approach for many patients.

Oral Precursors (NMN, NR)

Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) and nicotinamide riboside (NR) are oral precursors that the body converts to NAD+. Convenient. Some studies show modest effects on tissue NAD+ levels. Less peak effect than direct NAD+ administration. Often used as a bridge or maintenance strategy alongside periodic IV or subcutaneous dosing.

At Juvenis Medical, the choice depends on your goals: IV for intensive periods or first-time exposure, subcutaneous for ongoing maintenance, oral precursors as a daily complement.

Who Tends to Benefit Most

NAD+ therapy is most useful for patients who are:

  • Over 40 and noticing energy or cognitive decline that isn't fully explained by sleep or stress alone
  • Doing high-output work or training and feeling diminishing returns from recovery
  • Recovering from a major stressor — illness, surgery, prolonged travel, major life event
  • Already optimizing sleep, training, hormones, and metabolic health and looking for the next layer
  • Building a longer-term longevity protocol and want NAD+ as part of the foundation

Patients hoping NAD+ will fix sleep, hormone deficiency, untreated thyroid issues, or chronic inflammation as a single intervention are usually disappointed. NAD+ is a cellular tool — it doesn't override organ-level dysfunction.

NAD+ works best in the context of a complete protocol. Patients running NAD+ alongside hormone optimization, peptide therapy, and good lifestyle fundamentals tend to see significantly more benefit than those treating it as a standalone fix.

Safety Profile

NAD+ has a strong safety record. The most common side effects are infusion-related — flushing, chest tightness, mild nausea — and resolve when the infusion rate is slowed. Subcutaneous administration generally avoids these. Reported serious adverse events are rare.

Patients with active malignancy should discuss with their oncologist before starting; the same DNA-repair pathways NAD+ supports can theoretically support unwanted cells too. Pregnancy and active autoimmune disease are also reasons for caution.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly will I notice effects?
Energy and clarity improvements often start within 1–2 weeks of consistent dosing. Recovery and stress-tolerance benefits typically need 3–4 weeks. Long-term cellular benefits accumulate over months.

How often do I need NAD+?
Common protocols: a loading phase of 5–10 IV infusions over 2–4 weeks, followed by maintenance via subcutaneous self-injection 2–4× per week. Some patients prefer a quarterly intensive protocol with daily oral precursors in between. We tailor to your goals and schedule.

Is NAD+ available via telehealth?
Subcutaneous NAD+ for self-administration is a non-controlled compound and can be evaluated and prescribed via telehealth nationwide. IV infusions are done in person at the Oakland Park clinic.

Can I combine NAD+ with other therapies?
Yes — NAD+ stacks well with hormone optimization (TRT, BHRT), GH-releasing peptides (Sermorelin, Ipamorelin/CJC-1295), and recovery peptides like BPC-157. They work on different pathways and are commonly combined in longevity protocols.
